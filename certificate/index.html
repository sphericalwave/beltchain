Step 1

Academy gets verified

The gym registers its wallet. The registry admin (a federation, or us to start) marks it verified, so random wallets can't issue belts.

Step 2

Instructor records the promotion

The academy authorizes its black belts. One transaction writes name, belt, stripes, date and an optional link to the instructor's own black-belt record, building lineage on-chain. Cost on Base is a fraction of a cent.

Step 3

Anyone verifies

Scan the QR. The page calls fingerprint(recordId) and checks it against the printed hash. Edits to the paper break the match; revoked records show as revoked.

Decisions to make before this goes past a prototype. The first one matters most: the blockchain proves who recorded a belt and when, but it can't tell whether that person had the standing to award it.

Trust · most important

How does the global jiu-jitsu community know a gym is legit?

Right now one admin wallet (the deployer) verifies every academy. That works for a demo, but nobody will accept a single company as the gatekeeper of jiu-jitsu ranks.

  1. A federation verifies. The IBJJF or a national body holds the verifier key. They already keep a black-belt registry, so it's credible. But the sport is split: unaffiliated gyms, Gracie lines and no-gi organizations sit outside any one federation.
  2. Several verifiers; each reader picks whom to trust. Anyone can vouch for an academy: IBJJF, AJP, a national body, or an affiliation like Alliance or Checkmat. A tournament sets its own rule, such as "accept if IBJJF or our affiliation vouches." This is how browsers trust a list of certificate authorities rather than one.
  3. Lineage is the trust. A black belt is legitimate because of who promoted them. Seed a small set of recognized root black belts, then every later black belt traces back through "promoted by record #X" links. A fake gym can write records, but its chain ends at nobody. The contract already stores this link.
  4. Prove the wallet belongs to the gym. The gym posts its wallet address on its website or Instagram, or signs a message. This proves identity, not quality, so it complements the options above rather than replacing them.

Trust · bootstrapping

Who are the first root black belts, and will they sign?

Lineage only works once respected black belts are on-chain and have signed their students' promotions. It needs a few well-known professors to take part early. A natural first test is Alex's own lineage: who promoted them, and would that professor record it?

Privacy

Should student names be public and permanent?

Anything written to the chain can never be removed. Option A stores the name in plain text, so anyone can search it, like a public belt registry. Option B stores only a hash of the name, so the paper certificate carries the name and a verifier checks that it matches. Option B is friendlier for kids and for privacy laws, but you can't look someone up by name.

Operations

Who holds the keys, and what happens when one is lost?

Instructors need a wallet to sign promotions. Losing a key means losing the ability to issue or revoke belts under that identity. Options: a shared wallet that needs several people to approve, for academies; an easy wallet app with recovery for instructors; or a service that signs on their behalf, which brings back a middleman.

Adoption

How do existing belts get on-chain?

Decades of past promotions, including instructors' own black belts needed for lineage, have to be backfilled. That needs a bulk-import path, and a way to mark a record as "recorded later" rather than written on promotion day.
